    01 · What is UNIORA

    Your auth provider answers “who?”. UNIORA answers “what can they do, and where?”

    Authentication and authorization are different problems. You'll want to own the second one regardless of which auth provider you pick — or move to next year. UNIORA is the layer that sits between the two. It plugs into the provider you already use, and everything it manages lives in your database.

    Auth Provider

    “Who are you?”

    Supabase · Clerk · Auth0 · Better Auth
    U

    UNIORA

    “Which organization? What can you do? What has it unlocked?”

    in your own database
    ALLOWDENY

    Decision

    Never grants what it can't justify.

    deny-by-default
    ⌁

    Not an auth provider

    It never sees a password or a session. Your provider tells it who the user is; UNIORA decides what that identity may do inside an organization.

    ◈

    Not a SaaS

    No central UNIORA service holds your data. Organizations, memberships, roles, permissions and audit logs live under a dedicated uniora.* schema in your Postgres — or SQLite.

    ⬡

    Provider-agnostic

    Swap Supabase for Clerk, or run Auth0 alongside it, without rewriting authorization. Identity links even let a user migrate providers without losing their org, roles or permissions.

      03 · Mission & vision
      
      Authorization is a layer you own — not a feature you rent.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      
        
      Mission
      
        
      Give every product a clean, secure, provider-independent way to answer one question.
      
        
      “Given an identity and an organization, what may this person do — and what has the organization unlocked?”
      
        
      UNIORA exists so that teams stop re-implementing multi-tenancy, roles, invitations, feature flags and audit trails by hand — the place where privilege-escalation bugs are born — and instead get one tested, framework-independent core that keeps its data in their own database and refuses by default.
      
        
        
          
      • Secure by default: unknown, missing or inconsistent → DENY
        • 
          
      • Your data stays in your database — no central service
        • 
          
      • Explicit over implicit: membership is always verified
        • 
        
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Vision
      
        
      A world where changing your auth provider never means rewriting who can do what.
      
        
      Authentication is a commodity you should be free to swap. Authorization is the model of your business — organizations, responsibilities, capabilities, quotas — and it should outlive any vendor.
      
        
      The destination is a universal organization & authorization layer: every decision explainable, every change audited in a tamper-evident log, from a weekend prototype on SQLite to millions of memberships on PostgreSQL — and administrable by humans through a local-first Studio.
      
        
        
          
      • One model across Supabase, Clerk, Auth0, Better Auth and whatever comes next
        • 
          
      • Every decision explainable, every change audited
        • 
          
      • Resource-level permissions, role inheritance, per-org policies
        • 
        
      
      
      
    
      
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      
      04 · Principles
      
      Built like it will be attacked.
      
      
      Every guarantee below is enforced in code and tested — several of them with a real database constraint, not just application logic.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      
        01
        
      Deny-by-default
      
        
      The engine never grants access it can't confidently justify. Unknown permission, missing membership, wrong organization, suspended tenant: always DENY — never a silent fallback to ALLOW.
      
        can() → false
      
      
      
      
        02
        
      Organizations can't be orphaned
      
        
      Every organization is born with a protected Owner, created atomically. The last Owner can't be removed, blocked or demoted — enforced by a database constraint, so two simultaneous requests can't both win.
      
        last_owner ✕
      
      
      
      
        03
        
      Tamper-evident audit log
      
        
      Append-only and hash-chained: edit or delete an entry and verifyIntegrity() catches it. Every change made through Studio, invitations or identity links is recorded in the same transaction.
      
        sha256 ⛓ sha256
      
      
      
      
        04
        
      Organizations and entitlements
      
        
      Permissions answer “what can this user do”. Features answer “what has this organization unlocked”. Quotas count seats and reports atomically. Three different questions, modeled separately.
      
        permission + feature + quota
      
      
      
      
        05
        
      Framework-independent core
      
        
      @uniora/core has zero dependency on Next.js, Express or any database driver. Adapters plug in around it; storage adapters are held to one shared conformance suite.
      
        core ← adapters
      
      
      
      
        06
        
      Server-side is the only real auth
      
        
      React helpers like <Can> are UX sugar, deliberately headless. The browser never talks to your database; the server computes a bounded snapshot and independently authorizes every real operation.
      
        UI hides · server decides
